Question and model
------------------
The trial compares guaranteed amounts of $0, $250, $400, and $600 paid at Ironclad Contract acceptance. Each uses the SAME sampled starter acquisition, Game 1, Review, Month 1 units, Contract numerator, reserve, and lean Game 2. No new game rules or state were implemented. The $2,400 total cash cap is unchanged:

  upfront_cents = B
  completion_cents = floor((240000 - B) * numerator / 96)
  total_cents = upfront_cents + completion_cents
  B in {0, 25000, 40000, 60000}; numerator in [0, 96].

At numerator 0, the trial pays $0/$250/$400/$600. At numerator 48 it pays $1,200/$1,325/$1,400/$1,500. At numerator 96 all pay $2,400. This changes the completion incentive: the full 0-to-96 increase is $2,400/$2,150/$2,000/$1,800 respectively. This is a candidate, not the locked runtime payout.

The main cash timeline charges all sampled Game 1 Feature play at the beginning of that production as a conservative liquidity bound; advances each sampled Game 1 productive cycle; releases Game 1 with zero immediate sales; hires 0/1/2 staff before Contract acceptance at $0 and one productive cycle each; accepts Contract for zero cycles; applies upfront cash immediately; runs exactly two successful Contract hands; pays the completion remainder as the second hand's direct effect before its productive-cycle boundary; takes 0 or 2 one-cycle, $0 courses per staff after Contract; purchases the $450 Scope-2 reserve and optionally a $1,700 Store node at one cycle each; then advances sampled lean Game 2 production. One completed course raises payroll $25/month in this prototype. The candidate bill is $75/month and base salary $100/month per staff.

Every successful postrelease productive cycle earns Month 1 sales until its two-cycle cap. At each beginning-of-month cycle boundary, exact-cent earned-but-unsettled Game 1 sales, bill, and payroll are ONE net transaction. Sales can fund that same boundary's expense. No projected or unearned sale is spendable. No Game 2 or later-month sales are modeled. A negative cash outcome records the first shortfall; the rest of that trace is a counterfactual comparison, not a claim that gameplay would allow the failed action. Main timing has the first bill from Game 1 and newly hired staff/course raises effective at the boundary crossed by their successful action. Delayed alternatives are below.

Timing sensitivity and unresolved rules
---------------------------------------
For one staff/two courses without the optional node, main first-bill/same-hire/same-raise timing has shortfall 0.10% at $0 and 0.06% at $400. If the first bill begins only after release, it is 0.03% and 0.00%. If payroll begins at the NEXT boundary after hiring, 0.05% and 0.02%. If course raises begin at the NEXT boundary, 0.08% and 0.03%. With delayed bill, hire payroll, and course raises together, 0.02% and 0.00%. These variants are trial assumptions, not decided timing rules.

The first bill/payroll month, hiring action fee and cycle, whether a hire on a boundary joins that very payroll, course raise effective month, and insufficient-cash rollback remain unresolved. The one-cycle/$0 hiring and course assumptions are specifically trial inputs. The eventual implementation must preflight an exact-cent net monthly transaction atomically with the central productive-cycle boundary and preserve state on insufficient cash or overflow. This analysis records the first hypothetical shortfall but cannot prove a runtime rollback rule that does not yet exist.

Authority conflict and decision
-------------------------------
Canonical section 47 currently says all Ironclad cash scales continuously with exact completion. Any nonzero guaranteed amount pays cash at ZERO completion and therefore conflicts with that lock. The trial does not silently amend section 47. A design decision must explicitly replace or qualify that rule before implementation. The $400 candidate is not approved by this trial: it improves earlier liquidity modestly but reduces completion-dependent cash by $400 at full completion and leaves the difficult paths largely unaffordable. The total cap remains $2,400 in every candidate.
