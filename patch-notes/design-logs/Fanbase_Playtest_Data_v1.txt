METHOD
The script samples six free starter Features plus legal one-click Store purchases at printed Scope target 20, 21, 22, or 23, spending at most $4,000, with no phase minimum. Separate opt-in targets 7, 10, 13, 16, and 19 test the below-20 warning choice. It also samples a separate expanded later-game snapshot that owns all 27 Primitive Features (39 printed Scope); its acquisition cost and calendar path are outside this analysis.

Every project draws seven Design/Alpha candidates by the current sorted-ID weighted printed-score rule, with finite Features and renewable Core Passes. The policy selects four candidates per hand; redraw attempts replace same-type candidates, consume the shared budget, and successful hands replenish one. Printed Feature play costs are checked against starting cash after Store purchases. The port applies current Design/Alpha specialization and Balanced Production multipliers, Scope, Bug pressure and stochastic finalization, then samples seven-card Beta pools using the existing category/definition weights, QA search/debug, Balanced Operations and QA Specialization, and marketing output. Review uses the live 33-per-Core v2 standard, Scope and Bug penalties, variance, saved Genre Fit, and half-up one-decimal rounding. Month 1 units use the exact current floor formula, with a weighted market forecast. Fan sensitivity is computed only from actual two-cycle Month 1 units, never from launch projection as a runtime grant.

Modeled policies are explicit choices, not human-skill estimates: conservative = 2 Design + 2 Alpha hands, no redraw, random hand selection, no Beta hand; ordinary = 2 Design + 3 Alpha hands, up to one redraw/hand, Scope/printed-score selection, 2 Beta hands; optimized = 3 Design + 4 Alpha hands, up to two redraws/hand, projected score/Scope/balance selection, 4 Beta hands and roster-informed Genre choice. Design/Alpha priorities are 25/25/25/25. Ordinary Beta priorities are QA/Marketing/Insider 35/35/30; optimized uses 50/35/15. All are legal distributions.

MODEL LIMITS
This is a Python port of the referenced Godot rules, not the Godot PRNG or a direct runtime replay, so individual sequences are not bit-identical. The roster sampler is random greedy, not uniform over all legal rosters. Player hand/Genre/QA choices are modeled policies. The Beta model omits insider information, Host Playtest, and Beta redraws; those are not needed for the reported Review path but may affect decisions, Core corrections or marketing in a real run. Future Feature Store upgrades, employees, salaries, contracts, bills, publisher rewards, and player learning are excluded. The expanded all-Primitive pool is a supply snapshot, not a simulated acquisition calendar or cash forecast. Month 2+ fans and sales are not simulated. These limits make the results bounded sensitivity evidence, not predictions of human play.

FAN CANDIDATES, INTEGER ROUNDING AND 5.0 NEUTRALITY
Trial gain rate above 5.0: linear 0.08 × min(1.5,(Review−5)/2); square-root 0.08 × min(1.5,sqrt((Review−5)/2)). Gains = floor(actual earned Month 1 units × trial rate). At exactly 5.0, gains and losses are both zero even if units sold and marketing are positive. Below 5.0, trial losses = floor(min(pre-release fans, review-neutral potential reach) × 0.15 × (5−Review)), bounded by pre-release fans. Potential reach uses market and Awareness before the Review multiplier; this avoids a worse Review reducing its own loss exposure. These rounding and exposure choices are simulation assumptions, not implementation rulings.

At neutral market and zero existing fans: Review 5.0 sells 535/714 units with Marketing 0/100 yet changes fans by zero. Review 5.1 sells 546/728; linear gains 2/2, square-root 9/13. Review 7.0 sells 750/1000; both gain 60/80. Review 9.0 sells 964/1285; linear gains 115/154 and square-root 109/145. The square-root curve gives more near 5 but slightly less at 9 than the capped linear trial.

SATURATED AWARENESS, LARGE FANBASES, AND COMPOUNDING
Trial fan awareness = floor(150 × pre-release fans / (pre-release fans + 300)), snapshotted at launch; 0, 300, 1,000, 5,000 fans yield 0, 75, 115, 141 Awareness and approach a 150 cap. The locked organic 100, marketing and Awareness Scale 200 remain in the unit formula; only the fan-awareness term is varied. At neutral market and 1,000 pre-release fans, a 4/3/2 Review loses 150/300/450 fans in this trial, with either Marketing 0 or 100 because exposure is already capped at the fanbase. At 5,000 fans, Marketing 0 loses 165/330/495; Marketing 100 loses 202/405/608. No trial can take fans below zero.

Each curve/Marketing case runs 250 independent 20-release trajectories: first release from first-game samples, subsequent releases sampled with replacement from the separate expanded-pool distribution. Fans accrue only from actual Month 1 earned units after two cycles. The weak-release recovery check takes the trajectory's fanbase after its ninth release, applies one actually sampled first-game Review below 5, then samples three expanded-pool releases. It measures return to the pre-weak level rather than substituting a fixed Review sequence.

RECOMMENDATION AND OPEN DECISION
Do not lock or implement either fan-gain curve, loss threshold magnitude, or saturated-awareness constants from this batch. The governing 5.0-neutral rule is preserved, but none of 3,000 modeled first games crossed 5.0 and the 10–20-release trial mostly stays near zero fans. Before choosing a curve, obtain a small set of actual playtest traces (starter purchases, hands, redraws, Beta actions, Scope/Core/Bugs, Genre, Review), reconcile their distribution with this policy model, and decide whether first-game production/Review balance should be tuned in a separate milestone. Then compare linear and square-root gains on the resulting above-5 population, specify integer rounding and one-shot gain/loss timing, and re-run multiple-release exposure tests. Do not turn the candidate loss or saturation formula into runtime authority merely because this report computes it.
