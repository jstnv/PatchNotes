Trial mechanics (not locked)
- Search perk: once per project, the first successfully committed Search whose ordinary result leaves Hidden Bugs moves one additional Hidden Bug to Known. It cannot create a Bug.
- Debug challenge: two productive Searches and a Debug in a later hand. The later Debug receives at most one extra Known Bug fix if ordinary Debug leaves Known Bugs. It never fixes Hidden Bugs and never triggers twice.
- All comparisons use the locked one-decimal Review and Month 1 units formulas. Net revenue uses floor(units × 999 cents × 70 / 100). Marketing Output enters Awareness linearly in the current Month 1 formula. There is no employee cost, course, salary or hiring assumption.

Paired fixed-action effect, pooled across priorities, budgets and action policies (3,000 paired cases per cohort per perk)
- Legal 20–23: both perks add 0.676 useful discoveries and 0.119 useful fixes per case; remaining Bugs average 1.998 versus 2.315 without perks. Mean Review gain is 0.014 points; 86.4% are unchanged at one decimal. Mean Month 1 gain is 1.593 units and 1,114.217 cents of 70%-share net revenue. No sample crossed 5.0 or 7.0 upward.
- Below-20 opt-in: both add 0.365 discoveries and 0.036 fixes; remaining Bugs 1.077 versus 1.13. Mean Review gain 0.004; 96.2% unchanged. Mean gain 0.426 units and 297.668 net cents. No 5.0/7.0 threshold crossing.
- Expanded Primitive supply: both add 0.793 discoveries and 0.207 fixes; remaining Bugs 4.296 versus 4.465. Mean Review gain 0.043; 71.6% unchanged. Mean gain 4.808 units and 3,362.458 net cents. 1.3% cross 5.0 upward; none cross 7.0.
- Search alone produces most of the modeled effect. The Debug-only reward is useful in 11.9% of legal first-game cases, 3.6% of below-20 cases and 20.7% of expanded cases when both rewards are offered. Challenge completion is broader: 21.1%, 7.9% and 28.9%, respectively, because a later Debug may have no extra Known Bug to fix.
- The first-Search bonus prevented a second Search from being productive in 263/3,000 legal first-game cases, 282/3,000 below-20 cases and 81/3,000 expanded cases. This lowers challenge completion in the combined-perk path and must be addressed before locking a challenge/reward pair.

Availability and budget strata
- Initial Hidden Bugs 0: 24 legal first-game cases, 156 below-20; neither reward has an effect. At 1–2 Hidden Bugs, the extra Debug reward is useful in 0% of all three cohorts. At 3–4, useful Debug shares are 5.6% legal, 3.2% below-20, 4.0% expanded; at 5+, 19.3%, 16.9%, 22.7%. Full counts and one-decimal Review histograms are in the raw summary.
- Legal first-game QA-low / 2-hand fixed-action cases: combined reward has 0.012 mean Review gain, 88.4% unchanged; challenge completion 1.6%, useful Debug fix 1.2%. QA-high / 4-hand QA-first cases: challenge completion 48.4%, but only 17.6% obtain an extra fix and 94.0% still have unchanged one-decimal Review. Priority/action budget strongly changes opportunity, so pooled reward percentages alone are not a balance target.

QA-versus-Marketing action choices
- The QA-first and Marketing-first choice policies each spend the same 2 or 4 productive Beta cycles. They use the same sampled pre-Beta project, Review roll and market snapshot, but finite-card exhaustion makes their later candidate paths diverge. The comparison is a modeled action-policy sensitivity, not a prediction of human skill or a strictly fixed-draw causal estimate.
- Legal first-game, QA-low, 4 hands: QA-first yields 8.38 less Marketing Output/Awareness than Marketing-first, but 1.78 fewer remaining Bugs, 0.13 higher Review, 9.66 more Month 1 units and 6,752.44 more net cents on average. At QA-high, 4 hands, the modeled QA-first advantage narrows to 0.07 Review and 3.12 units while giving up 6.54 Marketing Output.
- Expanded Primitive supply, QA-high, 4 hands: QA-first gives up 9.46 Marketing Output/Awareness, fixes enough more Bugs for 0.50 higher Review and averages 43.78 more units. These are model results under the stated choice heuristic, not a proposed reward magnitude.

Boundaries and recommendation
- The reused production model ports current ledger weights, finite Feature/renewable Pass draws, priority weighting, Scope/Core synergies, Bug pressure, Genre Fit, Review variance and Month 1 sales. It approximates player choices with conservative/ordinary/optimized policies and omits UI timing, redraw decisions during Beta, insight utility, corrective Pass injection, contract plays, and concurrent releases. Store-era Features are excluded from the expanded cohort because their project play costs are still undefined. These gaps can change reward opportunity and policy ranking.
- The actual employee system is absent. No employee was created; no gameplay, Review, sales or authority formula was changed. Hiring price, salary, course cost, perk magnitude and permanence remain design decisions. A human playtest or a more exact Godot draw replay should precede any lock. In particular, consider a challenge that does not punish a permanent Search bonus for finding the last Hidden Bug.
