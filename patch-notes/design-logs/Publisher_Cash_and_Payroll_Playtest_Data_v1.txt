METHOD
6,300 launch-to-Game-2 scenarios = 150 samples each of seven purchased Scope targets (7, 13, 19 below-20 opt-in; 20, 21, 22, 23 target), three explicit play policies (conservative, ordinary, optimized), and two initial half-month alignments. Six free starters, legal starter purchases under the $4,000 and 23-Scope caps, and printed Feature play costs follow the current ledger. The existing fanbase_playtest_v1 Python port supplies seven-card weighted Design/Alpha draws, four-card selections, finite Features/renewable Passes, synergies, Bugs, Genre Fit, Review variance, and exact Month 1 unit floor. This batch ports Beta QA/Marketing again to include the newly implemented all-Marketing ×1.5 aggregate floor. It uses two Beta hands for ordinary, four for optimized, and none for conservative. The policy descriptions and the Python-vs-Godot PRNG limits are in Fanbase_Playtest_Data_v1.txt; they are not human-skill estimates.

Each scenario then draws two seven-candidate fixed Primitive Contract hands using the current equal 25/25/25/25 Core priorities, contract-specific primary-Core assignment, finite Features and renewable Passes. Conservative/ordinary/optimized policies attempt 0/1/2 same-type redraws per hand, respectively, with the existing four-redraw supply and one refill per successful hand. The four-card choice varies by policy. Scope and Core half-units use ContractState's ×1.0/×1.5 specialization rule; completion numerator = 4 × min(Scope,12) + sum(min(Core half-units,12)); exact payout = floor(240000 cents × numerator / 96). No Contract Feature play cash charge. Changed priority commits were not sampled, because the handoff defined no player policy for them; they would add a productive cycle and alter subsequent category draws.

Cash begins at $5,500 minus actual sample starter acquisition. The model conservatively charges the total sampled successful Game 1 Feature play cost before its production cycles. It starts the $75 bill at the first month crossing, hires 0/1/2 staff at Studio with zero fee/cycle (an explicit assumption), and pays salary at crossings after hire. Courses occur before Game 2, cost $0 directly, take one cycle each, and add $25 to that employee's salary from completion. Game 2 buys one legal unowned 2-Scope Primitive reserve for $450 and one cycle; the optional $1,700 Store node is a separate purchase/cycle cash stress only, without inventing its eligibility or card effect. Game 2 draws/plays a fresh owned pool. Month 1 earned sales use two cycles, cumulative half/full units and exact net cents; settlement occurs only at month boundaries. Default expense order at a crossing is sales settlement, then bill/payroll; a reverse-order sensitivity is reported. A negative modeled balance identifies the earliest unaffordable purchase, hand-cost bound, or bill; the simulation continues to measure shortfall rather than pretending the runtime accepted it.

TIMING AND VALUE SENSITIVITY
For one staff/two courses, lean Game 2 shortfall is 0.0% at a $50 bill/$75 base salary, 0.1% at $75/$100, and 6.3% at $100/$150 when settlement precedes expense. If bills/payroll are charged before same-boundary settlement, the last two rates rise to 2.2% and 9.0%. Delaying the first bill until after Game 1 changes the $75/$100 baseline from 0.1% to 0.0%. These differences make settlement order and bill start a necessary implementation ruling; the model has not chosen one for gameplay.

LIMITS AND RECOMMENDATION
This is a Python policy model, not direct Godot PRNG replay or observed human play. The roster sampler is random greedy, not uniform over legal rosters. Game 1 and Game 2 play costs are grouped up front as conservative liquidity bounds; a real player may distribute them over later hands. Beta omits Insider effects, Host Playtest, and Beta redraw, and the candidate Store node is a cost-only stress. Expenses/hiring do not yet exist in RunState, so failed expenses are hypothetical; the runtime never approved a negative balance. No employee ability, hiring fee or time, publisher Promotion, fanbase, Month 2+ sales, saving, alternative Contract offer, or changed-priority contract policy was invented. The current low Review distribution should be calibrated against actual traces before final pricing.

The $75/$100/$25 prototype appears viable for a lean Game 2 with zero or one employee under these modeled policies, but it does not support an automatic optional $1,700 purchase, and two employees with two courses are materially risky. Do not implement the expenses yet. First lock hiring time/fee, bill start, payroll start, same-boundary expense/settlement order, insufficient-cash handling, and course timing; then re-run with real player traces and a direct Godot replay fixture. Preserve the one-shot Ironclad cash anchor and explicitly define any additional publisher offers before using their hypothetical caps in gameplay.
