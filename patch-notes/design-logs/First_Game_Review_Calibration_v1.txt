Observed Review and model reconciliation
- Godot standard starter pools: all 36 Reviews below 5.0; conservative median 0.6 (range 0.2–0.8), ordinary median 1.5 (1.1–2.1), optimized median 2.45 (1.6–3.0). The highest was 3.0. Across the 36, average finished Scope ranged by policy from 15.5 to 21.2; average Core score from 13.3 to 21.3. The 9 opt-in low-Scope paths also stayed below 5.0. This is a small, policy-sensitive sample, not a population estimate.
- In the 3,000-path model, p10/median/p90 Reviews were 0.7/1.8/3.3; 100.0% below 5.0, 0.0% exactly 5.0, 0.0% above 5.0. Earlier Fanbase_Playtest_Data_v1 reported 0.7/1.7/3.3 and 100% below 5.0. The new seed and current Beta do not reverse that finding. Below-20 opt-in model paths: 0.6/1.5/2.8, also 100% below 5.0.
- All 45 Godot final Reviews and their production rating, Scope completion, Genre Fit and Genre deviation exactly matched the Python Review calculation when given the same resolved Godot scores, Scope, remaining Bugs, Genre, and variance roll. This establishes Review formula parity, not full draw-engine parity.
- Paired old/current Beta policy comparison across 3,750 model paths: 391 Marketing outputs changed due to Marketing specialization; zero remaining-Bug differences and zero Review differences. Marketing affects launch reach and sales but does not directly raise Review. This is a genuine first-game balance concern under these policy budgets, not a Review regression caused by later Beta work.
- Counterfactual mean Review gain across the 3,000 standard model paths, holding other resolved inputs fixed: set all Core scores to at least 33, +2.164; grant full Scope, +1.083; remove Genre Fit penalty, +0.209; fix all remaining Bugs, +0.121. These are diagnostic sensitivities, not proposed buffs. Low production scores and incomplete Scope dominate these paths. Correct Beta QA cannot compensate for them by itself.
- Legal-action scope is important: these policies intentionally play 2–3 Design hands and 2–4 Alpha hands, with 0–4 Beta hands. Longer or more skilful paths could reach higher scores. The result does not prove a mathematical first-game ceiling or how humans will play.
