Method, sample, and reproducibility
I used Godot 4.7.1 headlessly to instantiate the actual gameplay scene and drive its UI buttons/phase methods. The harness calls live draw, redraw, card selection, scoring, Review, RunState cash/calendar, Store, Contract, and sales code. It seeds phase RNGs and the existing snapshot/Review rolls. For Game 2 it reapplies the controlled snapshot rolls before the real preparation path because the public verifier hook is pre-tree only. No gameplay behavior is patched. The trace records every purchase, candidate draw, redraw, committed card set, printed and resolved Core/Scope, synergy, Bugs, Genre Fit, Review components, Awareness, Month 1 units, earned/settled cash, Contract, publisher status, and calendar/cash after each action. The three readable action ledgers show the date at each cycle; every new month begins on an even completed cycle.

Baseline: 110 first-game runs per policy, including 100 legal 20–23-Scope purchased pools and 10 separate 19-Scope opt-in pools. That is 330 first games, 300 standard and 30 low-Scope. Each policy also has 60 complete second-game continuations on 30 distinct seeds (QA and Marketing pairs), or 180 Game 2 releases total. Seed rule: 270927000 + 97 × floor(case / 2); QA and Marketing variants share each seed and pre-Beta state. Starter rosters vary three ways at each target, Genres rotate Action/Adventure/Strategy, and production focus includes Sound and mixed. All starter purchases stay under $4,000. Policies were fixed before the batch: cautious 2 Design/2 Alpha/1 Beta hands; ordinary 4/4/3; informed optimizer 6/6/5. They use different selection and redraw priorities. These are automated choices, not human play observations. “More hands” also means more cycles, so cross-policy Review differences are not a same-time skill comparison.

No visible desktop UI playthrough was possible in this environment. These are headless scene-control paths, not evidence that a human understands the menus or cards. The full project scene was instantiated repeatedly; this goes beyond a formula-only simulation but does not test pointer layout, hover/tooltips, or screen-reader presentation. First-game and Game 2 runs completed without a tested-route stall. Counts and failures are in overnight_current_playtest_v1_summary.json. The raw ZIP includes every JSON trace and three complete action-by-action runs; see overnight_current_playtest_v1_three_action_ledgers.txt for a readable version.

Low-Review finding investigated first
The current Review calculation uses four 33-point B Core standards, a 30-Scope denominator, a remaining-Bug multiplier, discrete variance, and Genre Fit. All 330 live first-game results recalculated exactly from their saved inputs. The prior 3,000-path fanbase model reported p10/median/p90 0.7/1.8/3.3 and 100% below 5.0. Its production budgets were 2/2/1 cautious, 2/3/2 ordinary, and 3/4/4 optimized, and its Python card draws/choices only approximate Godot. With those older ordinary and optimizer hand budgets replayed in current Godot on 30 matched starter/Genre/seed cases each, median Reviews were 1.7 and 2.45. The same current cases using 4/4/3 and 6/6/5 reached 2.4 and 3.55. The cautious current policy also uses 2/2/1. This identifies policy length and approximate draws/choice as the main cross-study mismatch; it does not reveal a Review formula regression.

The low scores remain a real current-build balance issue under ordinary budgets. First-game ownership has at most 23 printed Scope at confirmation, so the 30-Scope Review target cannot be completed from the starter pool. Matched strategy checks and bounded recommendations — unapproved candidates
1. Align early Scope opportunity with the Review target, or explicitly communicate the shortfall before setting a numeric change. The standard starter pool tops out at 23 while Review requires 30. Holding every actual resolved input fixed and changing only the first-game Scope denominator from 30 to 23 raises Review by p10/median/p90 0.3/0.7/1.3 across 300 legal starts. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a proposed locked 23-point rule; it still leaves ordinary median Review below 5. A Core standard of 30 instead of 33 adds only median 0.2, and 28 adds 0.4; changing the tuned Core standard alone is a weaker response and would need to preserve the user’s earlier 27/30/36/36 ≈7 target.
2. Make balanced Core production easier to recognize and test its category economics before changing cards. In 30 matched ordinary Godot seeds with identical roster, Genre, QA/Marketing route, and hand budget, mixed focus beat Sound focus by median 0.5 Review (mean +0.643) and median 55.5 Month 1 units (mean +70). Sound focus averaged Core 14.07/42.80/21.47/16.00; mixed averaged 22.80/18.27/26.00/24.40. The current Review imbalance penalty punishes the Sound spike. This supports a contextual warning/forecast or a bounded draw-weight trial; it does not yet prove a card-stat defect or authorize a rebalance.
3. Put a defined economic or calendar consequence around very long renewable-Pass production before expanding later sales systems. In 20 paired optimizer seeds, 20 Design plus 20 Alpha hands versus 6 plus 6 raised median Review by 1.85 at exactly 28 extra cycles; median cash at release changed $0 (p10 -$40). Long-run Review median was 5.35, p90 5.8, and none reached 7.0 because finite Scope still limits the result. This is not an infinite cash payout loop, but with no payroll/expenses and renewable Passes it is a practical score grind for time alone. Trial a bounded production-time cost or stronger diminishing return separately; do not implement or lock either from this automated evidence.

QA-versus-Marketing is not a universal winner. In 50 same-seed, same-pre-Beta pairs per policy, Marketing minus QA mean Review was -0.024 cautious, -0.242 ordinary, -0.188 optimizer. Ordinary Marketing raised Awareness by mean 14.7 but left 2.64 more Bugs, sold 13.88 fewer units, and left $97.06 less cash before Game 2. Optimizer Marketing raised Awareness by 22.3, sold 8.22 more units, and left $17.48 more cash. The four-Marketing specialization appears in these paths. The finite Playtest Rival Games action pays $1,000 once per project and exhausted correctly; no repeatable Contract or sales-cash arbitrage appeared. More cycles/Passes improve scores, but there is no direct repeat-cash loop in the available fixed Month 1 economy.

Ranked findings and player-confusion risks
1. High balance impact: a first-game purchased pool cannot reach the 30-Scope Review denominator, and usual hand budgets leave multiple Core scores below 33. This produces low Reviews even after all Bugs are fixed.
2. Medium impact: Sound-heavy play can look productive because one Core rises sharply while the Review rewards balance; Genre Fit adds another non-obvious multiplier. Mixed production won the bounded paired test.
3. Medium future risk: renewable Passes plus absent ongoing costs create a long-production score grind, though finite Scope and the one-shot Contract prevent the tested cash loop.
4. Medium communication risk: Publisher List unlocks can look like new work offers although only Ironclad has a playable contract. The Store’s branch price and familiarity information need human comprehension testing.
5. Verification limitation: Godot prints missing card-art placeholders and a Windows certificate warning in headless runs. The Contract verifier also prints a resource-at-exit diagnostic despite zero assertions failing. No corresponding gameplay stall was observed. A harness label error for 12 already-owned reserve attempts was caught by independent cash checks; the harness now chooses an unowned reserve, those cases were replayed, and final baseline traces/summary contain successful legal purchases. This was an analysis defect, not a Store behavior defect.
