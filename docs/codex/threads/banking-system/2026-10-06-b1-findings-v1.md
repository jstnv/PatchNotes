# B1 first-loan comparison — design evidence

2026-10-06 (America/Los_Angeles). **READ-ONLY ANALYSIS, not an approved loan or a gameplay test of borrowing.** The [native route driver](b1_native_routes_v1.gd), [runner](b1_run_native_v1.py), [comparison script](b1_compare_v1.py), and [machine-readable comparison](evidence/b1-comparison-v1.json) are retained here. Godot 4.7.1 ran the 32 routes against the current finance/credit implementation with no loan code. The route traces and run manifests are in `evidence/`.

## Accepted rules used as constraints

One productive cycle is half a month. Base funding is $5,500; trait selection can add a separate $200 receipt. Rent is $500 at each completed month. A first bank loan may be at most $500, requires actual positive settled sales and repayment capacity, permits only one active loan, and waives future unaccrued interest on early payoff. The rate, term, due convention, capacity formula, and score-based price remain **OPEN**. Cash cannot go negative; outstanding bills keep their type, original due, payment history and age. Only rent currently produces live bills.

## Method and limits

Four legal scripted first-release bands (cycles 12, 13, 14, 18) crossed two game strategies and two seeds, each with a native $5,500 start and a genuine `create_studio` $5,700 trait-selection start: **32 native no-loan routes**. The paired trait receipt was exactly $200 and left release cycles, reviews, settlements and later end cash exactly $200 above the corresponding base route. All 32 traces ended valid with no route discrepancy or script error. Route manifests record the exact script hash and process result. The analysis uses the current checkout's finance/credit code, including the locally repaired Alpha affordability guard.

The comparison then adds a hypothetical $500 receipt and scheduled bank outflows to the *same fixed native actions*. It refuses a quote with rent arrears or inadequate first-payment capacity and stops when modeled cash cannot pay an installment, would overdraw, or native rent arrears after issuance would change payment ordering. These are **overlays**, not native loan behavior: they do not simulate changed choices, bank UI, typed bank bills, interest recognition, repayment credit, or late-installment effects. The route ends before all 12 payments; remaining balances are obligations, not saved cash. No 6.2-rated first release was reproducible from these scripts.

## Native first-game pressure

| First release | First actual sales settlement | $5,500 cash / rent arrears at launch | $5,700 cash / rent arrears at launch | First-game Review range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cycle 12 | Cycle 14 | $1,280–$1,420 / $0 | $1,480–$1,620 / $0 | 3.8–4.9 |
| Cycle 13 | Cycle 14 | $1,140–$1,350 / $0 | $1,340–$1,550 / $0 | 4.2–5.9 |
| Cycle 14 | Cycle 16 | $640–$850 / $0 | $840–$1,050 / $0 | 4.6–6.5 |
| Cycle 18 | Cycle 20 | $0 / $210–$360 | $0 / $10–$160 | 6.4–6.8 |

Thus the late scripted routes already reach zero cash and overdue rent before first sales. Their Ironclad contract receipt clears rent before settlement; a loan gated on settled sales cannot rescue or cause that pre-settlement outcome. The exact human-observed cycle-18, 6.2-rated near-bankruptcy path remains unverified. The observed ~$1,150 Feature spend is a route average, not a charge inserted into these traces.

## Candidate offer comparison

| Item | This thread's proposal A | Task33 historical illustration B |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | $500 | $500 |
| Price | 1% of scheduled opening principal each month, half-up cents | 10% total quoted interest at neutral score, split over 12 installments for comparison |
| Term | 12 monthly principal installments | 12 monthly installments |
| First / final payment | $46.66 / $42.16 | $45.82 / $45.98 under this cent split |
| Full scheduled interest / total | $32.50 / $532.50 | $50.00 / $550.00 |
| First due after even-cycle issuance | `c+2` | `c+2` |
| First due after odd-cycle issuance | `c+3`, end of first full month | `c+1`, next completed month |

Illustration B's exact cent split is an analysis assumption, **not** specified by Task33. Both quotes use this thread's proposed capacity screen only so price/timing can be compared fairly: `floor(max(0, floor(mean(last up to three completed post-settlement months' settled net sales)) − $500 rent − live other recurring payments) × 25%)`; at first settlement the sample is one month and no other bill producers are live. The maximum A payment needs at least $686.64 settled sales; B needs at least $683.92. All 32 first-settlement quotes qualified: $201.92–$1,112.76 capacity, no rent arrears. This shows that these high enough first months do not distinguish the screens. It does **not** prove the one-month sample is safe for weak releases.

All 64 immediate-issuance overlays remained payable on their unchanged native actions. At immediate issuance every first settlement was an even completed-month boundary, so both first dues matched. The 13-cycle release did not cause an odd settlement: it settled at cycle 14. Eight further native 13-cycle routes returned to Studio at odd cycle 15 after a second Ironclad hand; delayed issuance there produced 16 more payable overlays. A then first falls due at cycle 18; B first falls due at cycle 16. At cycle 16 A keeps $45.82 more cash than B. A costs $17.50 less full-term interest. No modeled installment was missed, so the existing bank-bill age and late-credit behavior cannot be validated by this replay.

First-month sales are a weak durability signal. On the 16 distinct strategy/seed paths, the rolling three-month proposed capacity later fell to **zero in every route** for at least four completed months, yet the fixed routes retained enough cash to keep paying. The offer should disclose the short sample and all scheduled payments; a declining sales screen must block a *new* quote without changing a previously accepted debt. In these fixed overlays interest never flipped a native profitable month to a loss/break-even month, and no payment missed; modeled monthly credit would therefore match the native score on these paths. Native end scores ranged 604–609. This is an inference from the prototype credit rule, not an executed bank-credit result.

## Design recommendation and open questions

Carry **proposal A** forward for user review: its first-full-month due is easier to explain when a player borrows between month boundaries, its $32.50 full-term interest is transparent, and $500 arrives only after the early pacing pressure has already played out. Do not lock it yet. The model supports affordability on these routes but offers no weak first-sales quote, real 6.2 late route, or adaptive player behavior. A separate weak-settlement rejection test, odd-cycle issuance/due test, and due/partial/arrears integration checks belong in the eventual implementation gate.

The user still needs to decide the fixed $500 amount, 1% monthly price, 12-month term, first-full-month due, 25% one-to-three-month capacity formula, and whether credit affects price or eligibility. Keep the existing rent-first, interest-before-principal, original-due aging and future-interest waiver as the proposed integration shape pending a ruling. No task from this record goes to the shared TODO without an explicit prompt.
