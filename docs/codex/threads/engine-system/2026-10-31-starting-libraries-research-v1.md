## Economics and progression

Current sales source prices a game at $9.99 per unit and retains 70% after platform share. Proposed royalty is on that platform net. At equal sales, Public gives the player 70% of platform net; either licensed engine gives 85%, after its $4,000 fee. The extra 15 percentage points repay $4,000 after **$26,666.67 platform net**, or **3,814 units** at $9.99 and the current 70% platform retention (rounded settlement difference $4,000.70). This is a cumulative same-sales threshold, not a career forecast.

As an illustrative first-month only projection, setting awareness and market multipliers to 1.0 in the current 500-unit-at-review-7 formula and applying each of the 100 optimized specialty reviews yields average Public Action **322 units / $1,578 player receipts**, Rival Puzzle **357 / $2,123**, and Rival Adventure **371 / $2,206** per release. The licensed royalty portions, about **$375 Puzzle** and **$389 Adventure** for these modeled months, would be paid to their creator rivals along with the $4,000 license fee. Matching genre instead, Public yields about **$1,687** for Puzzle and **$1,509** for Adventure, so the proposed license fee is recovered after roughly **10 Puzzle** or **6 Adventure first-month-equivalent releases** at these modeled quality differences. This omits later-month sales, production spend, ongoing rent, marketing, market factors and portfolio effects. The equal-sales threshold above is the reliable break-even comparison. At the current $5,500 base new-Studio cash, a $4,000 license leaves $1,500 before development costs and the current $500 monthly rent; early affordability is a significant risk even where lifetime royalty economics are better.

The approved proprietary comparison is $100 creation, 20 starting Scope, no royalty, per-engine implementation and an 80% maximum effective Studio familiarity discount plus an independent Skill Tree. The current simulator does not implement those costs, expansion milestones or required engine configuration, so a defensible numerical proprietary review distribution or cash break-even is **NOT RUN**. Its simple cash cost against Public is `$100 + actual implementation cost` versus 30% of platform net, with lower initial capacity and time costs unknown. Against licensed, it saves the $4,000 upfront fee and 15% royalty, but implementation/Skill Tree costs and review-driven sales can reverse that. No proprietary performance claim is inferred from the primitive 20-Scope foundation alone.
