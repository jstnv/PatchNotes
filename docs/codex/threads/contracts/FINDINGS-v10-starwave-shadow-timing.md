## Recommendation and limits

**Continue developing a cash-first, three-hand Starwave offer as a proposed bounded pilot; keep the amount, advance, completion formula and Promotion OPEN.** The three-cycle commitment has a real early cash and release-timing cost. Direct cash drives most of the observed late cash recovery; 6 or 14 extra Promotion adds a smaller settled-sales effect while stacking with Neon's 18 to produce 24 or 32 next-launch points. This supports testing a modest Promotion component before adopting the historical 14, but it does not select 6, $2,240 or $3,000 as rules. The high starting cash means this route says nothing about an acceptance advance as a rescue tool.

The full-cap fixed receipts are optimistic upper-bound inputs. An [older modeled study](../../../../patch-notes/design-logs/remaining_publisher_contracts_rebaseline_v1_summary.json) on revision `606f011` saw only **4 of 90** three-hand Starwave outcomes reach its historical full payout; its formula and economy are not current authority. V10 does not freeze or draw a Starwave-owned card pool, score a third hand, commit a Starwave result, validate a checkpoint after that result, or test player preference. Before choosing numeric terms or handing off implementation, the next read-only step is a current-source **three-hand card feasibility screen**: from this legal post-Neon checkpoint, freeze the eligible Primitive pool, use seven candidates with three retained and four replacements before each later hand, preserve finite Feature exhaustion and renewable Passes, and report attainable Scope/Core/completion distributions under declared policies. A separate legal low-cash foundation would be needed to judge any acceptance advance. No gameplay, shared To Do List or shared design-authority edit was made here.
