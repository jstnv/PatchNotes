The proposed direct-cash transaction accepts the offer at zero cycles, paying `A` cents once when `A > 0`; Neon at `A = 0` records acceptance with no cash transfer. Only committed hand 2 pays `floor((C − A) × n/d)` cents, where `n/d` is the clamped fixed-share completion. Zero completion totals `A`; full completion totals `C`; no recoupment or second advance. Promotion earns `floor(P × n/d)` whole points on completion, keyed by offer, then adds once to the next successful launch's Awareness. Neither pending offers nor failed/duplicate actions pay or consume it. The [current candidate formulas](../../design/publishers-contracts.md) supply `n/d`; the numerical targets and caps above require a user ruling before they become design authority.

## Why this package, and where it is uncertain

The [Task 34 findings](FINDINGS-v3-task34-advances.md) give Crown hand-pair completion counts of 432/528 at $0, 492/528 at $150 or $200, and 528/528 at $300. The final 36 rescued by $300 include adverse legacy routes that skipped an available Ironclad offer; Ironclad's existing $400 acceptance cash completed all 54/54 legal alternatives. This favors $150 as targeted help rather than a universal bailout. It is still conditional on the studied policies. The genuine $5,700 historical replay already clears its specific $147.65 rent boundary at $0, while other current-start early cohort arms benefit from $150. Neon advances did not improve access and increase average partial-completion cash, so $0 preserves its later marketing role.

The cash caps preserve the direct-payment ordering Ironclad $2,400 > Crown $1,920 > Neon $1,560 > one SideStreet $1,200. These are different offers with different timing and eligibility, so the ordering is a design reason, not a measured publisher preference. The cap sweep is arithmetic on fixed completed hands; altered second-hand cash might change later rent, credit or decisions. Pilot values need focused integrated-source route verification before any implementation claim and later human balance review before a final lock.

Promotion has the largest uncertainty. In Task 34's early $0-advance arms, Crown's candidate Promotion raised settled sales by a mean $300.57 at the observed horizon, with 168/216 positive; Neon raised them by a mean $751.17, with 96/96 positive. These are conditional fixed-action continuations, not live rewards or independent player samples. A Game 4 acceptance can add Promotion to Game 5's launch, but that recorded horizon ends before Game 5 earns; no future cash was treated as spendable. A small cap sweep varies magnitude on two legal routes only. Its Crown full-completion 0/6/12/18 points produce $0/$195.81/$405.60/$601.40 settled increment through the recorded horizon; Neon awards 0/9/19/28 points under nominal caps 0/10/20/30 and produces $0/$307.69/$629.37/$937.06. These route-specific values justify a bounded pilot, not a final rate or a cross-publisher comparison. On the swept Crown early route, the next launch's frozen Awareness is 111/117/123/129; only the 18-point arm crosses Neon's ≥125 profile gate if future Promotion enters the live launch snapshot. The overlay did not create a live Neon offer. Final Promotion balance needs a legal post-Game-5 earning and settlement window and later Fanbase integration checks.

## Structural rules and implementation dependencies

- **Already accepted direction:** one non-expiring first-unlock offer for each publisher; pending offers coexist; one active Contract; two four-card productive hands; owned Primitive Features plus Passes frozen at acceptance; no abandonment or minimum-completion gate; distinct zero-cash completions count. Promotion banks by offer without a silent global cap and is consumed only by the next successful launch. Ironclad/SideStreet cash rules stay as implemented. These structures are not Crown/Neon runtime today.
- **Neon focus remains OPEN until the user rules it:** the proposed automatic pilot rule matches Task 34's largest-owned-primary heuristic and must be visible in the offer before acceptance. The [192 fixed-hand re-scores](sim-v4-focus/FINDINGS-v1.md) found all recorded automatic focuses were Graphics. Retrospective Technology focus adds mean $90.70 direct cash and 1.5 conditional Promotion points, while the worst focus loses mean $556.28 and as much as $906.98 on one arm. Those hands have only six distinct final score profiles; an acceptance-time player cannot know their future result, and no redraw or finance continuation was rerun. Player override with a clear scoring preview is a separate candidate, not part of the first proposed pilot.
- **Launch integration proposal:** add banked Promotion to other positive launch Awareness inputs before the one final frozen value drives history, sales and publisher unlocks; apply active Studio Traits' existing first-launch penalty at its established place. Clear the bank only after successful launch commit. A failed launch, canceled flow or duplicate callback preserves it. This ordering should be reconciled with the current Studio Traits branch before implementation.
- **SideStreet prerequisite trap:** current `RunState._meets_publisher_requirement` checks aggregate completed Contracts for SideStreet. That is equivalent to Ironclad-first only while Ironclad/SideStreet are the sole live Contracts. When Crown/Neon count as completed, SideStreet must explicitly require committed Ironclad completion; Starwave still counts distinct completed Contracts. Current `ContractState`, result recorder and Contract screen also have Ironclad/SideStreet-specific payout, identity and text paths. New IDs cannot safely fall through those branches.
- **Persistence gate:** current checkpoint work is codec/storage foundation without gameplay hydration/Continue. Future offer identity, acceptance receipt, completed result, pending Promotion and next-launch consumption must roundtrip through the actual Studio checkpoint coordinator before claiming restart safety. No mid-hand save is needed under the current checkpoint scope.
