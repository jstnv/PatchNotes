## Matched outcomes

Buy+chain launched Game 4/5 at cycles **75/93**; no-buy/no-chain launched them at **68/86**. At each common endpoint, buy+chain was therefore **seven Game-6 work cycles behind**. At cycle 97 the buy arm had three Design hands into Game 6; no-buy had six Design plus four Alpha hands. No original bill or Bank principal remained unpaid in either arm at the three endpoints. Role-Playing's buy arm had an earlier late `rent:6` bill at Game-1 launch, settled in that same cycle after the real Ironclad receipt; no-buy did not. This is a cash-pressure cost of the purchase route, even though it did not block a planned hand.

## Recommendation and limits

**Retain the current Crown, Neon and Starwave ACTIVE/TRIAL terms and starter prices; no implementation or To Do List change.** This sample shows an economically useful *legal* access package for four specialties by cycle 97, despite the early cash squeeze and seven-cycle Game-6 lag. It does not show an optimal route or a universal cash win: Role-Playing was behind at cycle 93, its early rent was late, and the later work gap could matter after cycle 97. The source predates Education, all four cohorts use one seed and one trait/selection/shopping policy, and the arms differ in both Store ownership and publisher uptake plus a conditional loan. No independent causal effect of starter buying, Crown Promotion, Neon Promotion or Starwave payout can be read from the package delta.

If a numerical retune is contemplated, the next targeted evidence is a **buy+skip-later-Contracts** bridge on the same four foundations, matched to these endpoints. It would separate the Store route from incremental publisher uptake more cleanly. That is a proposed read-only study, not an approved balance rule or shared-list task. Player discovery of starter buying belongs in the Feature Store thread; this Contracts folder retains the access and economic evidence.
