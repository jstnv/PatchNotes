# 2026-10-09 opening Review gate failure and decision options

**Historical decision brief: G1 failed; choice A was later selected.** The [superseding user ruling](2026-10-09-opening-rule-revision-decision-v1.md) removes universal strict incumbent Review ordering and separately locks a nonperfect player first-game target. This brief preserves the evidence and alternatives as presented before that ruling. The implementation task was separately dispatched after the TODO listing and stopped at the opening gate. Its [source-matched findings](../../findings/ai-competitors-gates-v1/README.md) establish a legal first player release at frozen **10.0 Review**, with **$840 cash and no arrears**, reproduced in two fresh Godot processes and independently audited. Ordinary Reviews cap at 10.0. Thus the prior requirement that each incumbent's opening release score *strictly above every legal player first release* is impossible on current rules. The earlier [opening decision](2026-10-09-opening-delegated-decisions-v1.md) explicitly called for a new design ruling in this case.

The earlier definition of an incumbent's opening game **is already selected**: its first actual release on or after the January-1980 run start; pre-run titles are historical. The unresolved decision concerns the **universal Review ordering**, not that definition or the rule that rival Design, Alpha and Beta work must use actual legal hands. The prior assertion that the player first game cannot reach 10.0 is contradicted by the current-source witness. This does not itself authorize a gameplay rebalance.

## Choices to rule

| Choice | Rule and consequence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Strong legal incumbents, exceptional player may match or beat them (recommended)** | Keep incumbent prior titles, stronger Feature access and separate resources. Their post-start games still use actual legal hands and ordinary Review scoring. Remove the universal strict Review advantage; a rare legal first player game can tie or surpass an incumbent. Do not add a hidden rating bonus or lower the player's cap. | **SELECTED later**, with a separate locked target that player first games cannot reach 10.0. The mechanism for that target is OPEN. |
| **B — Legal 10.0 incumbent opening releases; player may tie** | Require every designated opening release to reach 10.0 through a proved legal hand path and an explicitly pinned deterministic route or equivalent guarantee. The player can tie at the cap. | **NOT SELECTED.** Keeps a maximum opening score, but needs a separate proof for every incumbent and may overproduce perfect early releases. |
| **C — Preserve strict superiority by rebalancing the player start** | Keep the original universal ordering, but separately change player starting access, economy or scoring so every legal first release falls below an incumbent's proved minimum. | **NOT SELECTED.** A player balance change is still needed for the separate nonperfect first-game target, without universal incumbent ordering. |

The later user ruling, not the G1 study, earlier recommended-choice delegation or implementation dispatch, selected A. The original universal strict ordering is now **SUPERSEDED**. The 10.0 witness remains a current-source balance gap under the separately locked player first-game target. G2–G5 remain separately **OPEN**. The market's effect on Reviews remains a separate design topic.
