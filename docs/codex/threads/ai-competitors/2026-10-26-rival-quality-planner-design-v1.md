# Rival quality planner — design proposal, 2026-10-26

**Scope authority:** The later [hard release-gate ruling](2026-10-26-rival-full-scope-hard-release-gate-decision-v1.md) makes actual full project Scope mandatory for every AI rival. Any blocked or short-pool state in this proposal remains unreleased until a legal full-Scope route exists.

**Phase authority:** The later [whole-supply Feature ruling](2026-10-26-rival-complete-feature-exhaustion-phase-gate-decision-v1.md) requires playing all possible Features in each phase's finite supply, not just the visible deal used by the planner below. Its legal search and recovery remain OPEN.

**Status: PROPOSED system; numerical policy and adoption OPEN.** The user's quality aim is ordinary first live rival games around 5.6–6.2, with no 2.x opening Reviews; several 8.0+ frozen historical games serve the initial reputation challenge. The [four-seed cause audit](2026-10-26-low-review-cause-finding-v1.md) shows that the live trial's weak four-Core output is the first bottleneck. This design seeks higher **calculated** Reviews through legal production, not a Review floor, Core grant on release, softer market standard, or free QA.

The subsequent [35-Scope roster-only screen](2026-10-26-35-scope-roster-screen-finding-v1.md) improved all eight first Reviews but left all eight below5.6, with one first game still under required Scope and one fewer live title by cycle24. It confirms that printed Scope alone is not the capability test; the project needs a legally attainable four-Core mix, actual draw recovery and a publication plan.

In that screen, Wayfinder's first-game **Design Core was only 24–30 against Adventure's base 53**, even when it played 33–35 Scope. Arcade's Graphics was 22–30 against Action's base 40 in all four first games, and Technology was 23–32 against base 40. These are targeted capability deficits for opening roster/Store planning. The numbers describe observed releases; they do not identify or approve a specific purchase or grant.

## 1. Plan capability before beginning a project

At Studio, form a finite project plan from the rival's actually owned, era-legal and prerequisite-legal Features, separated into Design and Alpha. Estimate at least one jointly playable hand grouping using the ordinary four-card rules, exact synergy resolver, finite card use, quoted Feature spend and known month-end dues. Track (a) total printed eligible Scope, (b) achievable played Scope, (c) plausible four-Core vectors, and (d) cash runway including an explicit QA reserve. The printed `35` candidate pool is a useful *supply check*, but it is not a quality or affordability proof.

Compare planned Core vectors with the **four frozen market standards for the chosen project Genre**. If the project cannot in principle reach required Scope, do not start an impossible project. Improve the owned pool through the already locked earliest-era/cheapest-within-era Store rule and an affordable plan, or defer the start with a recorded reason. An opening incumbent's stronger cards, bankroll and Fans need typed historical provenance; exact values and card IDs remain OPEN. The current Wayfinder's 27 printed Scope cannot pass this admission gate without a legal roster change.

The planner should aim for a defensible no-variance Review band using the ordinary calculator and a declared range of *publicly estimated* remaining Bugs. It may rank plans by their expected quality; it must never assign the target score to a release. An unfavorable draw, cash shock or variance can still produce a weaker actual game.

## 2. Choose Design and Alpha hands against the real target

Preserve the LOCKED rule to play the maximum legal, affordable visible Feature count in every hand. Among equally maximal legal four-card sets, first reject any choice that makes full Scope impossible with known remaining supply; then prefer the best attainable four-Core/Genre-target fit after exact hand synergy, especially the largest normalized Core shortfall, then lower actual cost and a stable token tie break. Use only current visible candidates, owned unplayed inventory, public cash and the project's frozen market standards. Do not inspect future draws or hidden Bugs.

The current selector evaluates `PrimitiveReviewCalculator.get_genre_profile`, which uses **base Genre targets**, while actual Review uses frozen project market standards. Give the AI a validated read-only view of those frozen standards. A seven-card deal has only 35 possible four-card sets, so exact current-hand enumeration is bounded; the across-project feasibility search and population CPU limit remain OPEN and require measurement. Do not add unbounded lookahead or a per-cycle four-Feature fiction.

Continue legal Feature hands through both phases. Design may exit below required Scope only when no legal Feature is currently available and Alpha supply can still close the gap. Alpha must not confirm an under-Scope exit. If owned unplayed supply can cover a shortfall but is absent from the visible deal, use a finite, funded ordinary redraw/Pass search; if genuinely impossible, retain an explicit blocked project and recovery path. Exact search allowance and distress handling are OPEN.

## 3. Protect achieved quality in Beta

Replace the trial's fixed two-Beta-hand exit with a finite opportunity decision. Estimate the expected Review gain from a legal Search→Debug opportunity using observed Known Bugs and a bounded hidden-Bug estimate, then compare it with one productive cycle, known bills and post-launch reserve. Prefer useful QA before Marketing as LOCKED. Once no defensible funded QA hand remains, take legal Marketing opportunities that improve reach without jeopardizing the reserve; Marketing Output is not bank cash and does not raise Review. Launch through ordinary Beta finalization when another hand has no justified expected value. Exact estimator, reserve, search/draw and stop rule are OPEN.

After each legal launch, record Core versus frozen target, Scope, Bugs, hand choices, costs and Review. Use the observed *category deficit* to guide the next affordable Store/Core plan. This is deterministic local planning data, not an adaptive Review multiplier.

## Test order and adoption gate

1. Source-match the current two-rival control with fresh legal starts. In a new private simulator variant, compare **A** current policy, **B** legal opening capability/resources alone, **C** B plus market-target hand planner/full-Scope admission and exit, and **D** C plus funded Beta decision. Keep historical 8.0+ titles outside active market pressure. Preserve each variant's baseline/ancestry and regenerate legal prefixes across gameplay revisions; equal seeds alone are insufficient.
2. Declare seeds, no-loan cash rule, exact opening card/resource candidates, player policy, fixed calendar horizon, command/release limits and stopping conditions before running. Include the existing four diagnostic seeds, a low-cash case, a short-supply case and a market-raised-target case. Report every blocked, censored, rejected and timed-out case. Measure first and later Reviews *with* four Core, Scope, Bugs and actual publication dates/count. A provisional design screen is no 2.x first releases in the declared ordinary cohort and a typical first-live result near 5.6–6.2; passing it does not guarantee or approve balance.
3. Check the player's same-calendar Review, sales and cash. Earlier legal long-hand candidates improved rival quality but cut publication count and eased player sales; a quality candidate cannot be adopted as a market-challenge fix if it repeats that result without a separately tested population/WIP response. Run focused legal/checkpoint tests, representative fresh-process replay, Studio-only Continue and CPU/save measurements before a handoff.

The 1980-only top-Review leadership wish, new-save 1979 work-in-progress provenance, exact cohort composition, entrant cadence, publication capacity, market coefficients, news and leaderboard remain separate OPEN design work. **No canonical gameplay change, implementation queue or numerical approval follows from this proposal.**
