# AI Competitors market pressure and rankings — design direction, 2026-10-17

## Authority and status

**LOCKED user direction, 2026-10-17:** Actual AI releases should make the market feel crowded and challenge the player. Rival output should affect the four Core standards used by future Reviews, alongside the existing project Genre targets. The number of market games sharing Genres and Themes should affect sales. A later leaderboard should compare games by highest Review and sales. This explicitly opens the market-on-Review topic previously kept separate in [world decision W7](2026-10-09-rival-world-delegated-decisions-v1.md); that earlier separation governed the foundation and does not prohibit this newly requested system. The rival's own Review still comes from legal Design, Alpha, Beta and launch work, never from an independent rating roll.

**CANDIDATE architecture for the next design/implementation slice:** The rules below are the recommended structural model. They are not implemented or numerically approved. Existing Genre target values, sales coefficients and frozen release records remain ACTIVE/TRIAL until a source-pinned handoff adopts a specific delta. The market's exact influence, age window, caps, hand policy and challenge targets remain **OPEN**.

## One market, two effects

1. **Monthly market snapshot.** At a central calendar month boundary, take one deterministic snapshot of live releases across the player, AI incumbents, entrants and any continuing former player Studio. A release made during that month enters the *next* monthly snapshot. This prevents player/rival action order and same-month launch timing from changing each other's market pressure. Pre-1980 frozen archive titles are excluded. The snapshot has a stable month identity, release identities and source/formula version; Continue restores the committed snapshot rather than recomputing it under different source.
2. **Recent presence.** Saturation uses a bounded recency window based on release age, independent of earned units. Zero-unit titles cannot remain competitors forever just because a campaign might revive their own sales. The active window and age weights are **OPEN**. A title cannot compete with itself. Other titles from the same Studio can compete for its buyers; for Review standards, compare against *other Studios* so a studio's own prior hit does not alone raise its next project's bar. Both player and rival studios face this rule symmetrically.
3. **Core standards for new projects.** A project's four Review standards begin with the existing Genre-specific target vector. Recent other-studio releases of the *same project Genre* may lift individual categories using their frozen, actual four-Core outputs. A weak release must not lower a Genre baseline or impersonate a strong benchmark. Freeze the resulting four-category vector, market snapshot ID and formula ID when legal development begins; display it before hand choices. A later rival release does not rewrite an active project's target or any released Review. The exact qualifying quality, aggregation, lifts, caps and missing-Core compatibility rule are **OPEN**. Store planning against permanent specialty remains a separate AI choice calculation.
4. **Genre/Theme sales crowding.** For each earning title, count other recent titles in three mutually exclusive groups: same Genre only, same Theme only, and same Genre+Theme. The shared monthly snapshot supplies those counts. A pair match should create at least as much pressure as a one-field match, with a lower bound so competition cannot produce negative units; exact weights/caps are **OPEN**. Apply a distinct saturation factor alongside the existing frozen launch forecast, Review and Awareness inputs. Freeze that factor for each two-cycle release-age sales month, then use the newest committed market snapshot for the next age month. Previously earned units and cents never change. The title's own older releases may cannibalize it, as other studios' titles may.
5. **Visible provenance.** Pre-Development should show base Genre targets plus any current market lift; active Review detail should show its frozen target source. Studio/market information should show the current Genre/Theme counts and age window. Sales reporting should identify the frozen forecast and the separate saturation factor used for that age month. No hidden retroactive penalty or undocumented rank boost.

The monthly snapshot is a market input, not a duplicate owner of sales. `RunState`/`ReleasedGameSales` remain authoritative for each title's actual Review, earned units and exact-cent settlement. Use stable `(studio_id, release_id)` identity in cross-studio indexes; never infer a market release from a historical archive or from a projected/failed launch. Save each month factor or sufficient versioned inputs to validate and replay it, including failed/cancelled and Continue boundaries.

## Later leaderboard

**CANDIDATE presentation:** Provide separate all-time **Highest Review** and **Most Units Sold** rankings for real post-start player and AI releases. Review uses frozen one-decimal results; sales uses cumulative *earned* units, never a launch projection or gross forecast. Show Studio, title, Genre, Theme, release date, Review and earned units, with a deterministic tie order. Historical archive titles can remain in the rival's history but lack actual post-start sales, so they do not enter the live leaderboard. Net settled revenue can be displayed as a separate fact, not substituted for units or mixed into a made-up combined score. Exact board size, unlock, UI location, tie policy and whether to add current-month charts remain **OPEN**.

## Alternatives retained

| Choice | Recommended candidate | Alternative retained / reason deferred |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | One month-start snapshot, later releases affect the next month | Per-action recalculation introduces player/rival order bias and can change a target during an unresolved hand. |
| Review target | Freeze at project start; lift from recent other-studio actual Core in the same Genre | Launch-time or phase-by-phase targets surprise a project after its choices. All-time upward ratchet makes eventual play impossible. |
| Presence | Bounded age-based live window | Count every past title forever (permanent saturation); qualify by sales (circular because saturation itself changes sales). |
| Sales | Recompute at each release-age month start, freeze for its two earning cycles | Launch-only crowding never responds to later releases; every-cycle crowding breaks a monthly unit promise. |
| Leaderboard | Separate frozen Review and earned-unit boards | A combined score needs arbitrary weighting and obscures what the player actually beat. |

## Source reconciliation and implementation order

Current source has a shared `RivalWorld.live_releases` record for rivals, while the player's and every Studio's release accounting lives in `RunState`. The world summary omits four-Core output, but `RunState.get_release_metadata(id).review.cores` already freezes each released title's uncapped four-Core vector and `standards`; the market index can reference that validated owner metadata without duplicating it. Missing-Core handling is still needed for older/partial records. `PrimitiveReviewCalculator` obtains fixed Genre targets from `GENRE_TARGETS`; its project profile is reconstructed from current rules. `PrimitiveUnitsSoldCalculator` freezes Month 1 units, and `ReleasedGameSales` recalculates later units using frozen `market_bp`; it has no age-month saturation history. Theme currently identifies a project but does not enter those two sales calculators. A truthful implementation therefore needs a versioned cross-studio index, project-frozen market standards, monthly snapshots, a separately audited age-month sales factor, checkpoint migration, and UI explanations. Do not bolt a multiplier onto `RivalWorld.live_releases` or retroactively rewrite existing title Reviews/sales.

Recommended order: (1) source-pinned read-only market/release audit and historical compatibility plan; (2) pure snapshot/index over existing owner metadata; (3) improve legal rival card choice/Genre/Theme variety enough to produce credible competition; (4) project-frozen Review standards; (5) age-month sales ledger extension; (6) Studio explanations and the separate leaderboards; (7) bounded matched-route and human challenge review. The [one-seed baseline](2026-10-17-market-baseline-finding-v1.md) observes current two-hand/fixed-Theme Reviews of 1.6–2.9, so using those trial releases to set market pressure before improving the rivals risks punishing the player for weak competition. Each step needs exact replay, rejected-action/rollback, month-boundary, cold Continue and old-save cases appropriate to its schema. No successful headless result alone approves final balance.

## OPEN decisions and acceptance

- Market age window/weights and release eligibility, how an old release lacking frozen uncapped Core contributes to Review standards, and migration of active projects and sales ledgers.
- Quality gate, four category aggregation/lift/caps and source/profile IDs; sales weights, caps/floor, rounding, month crossing and any Genre/Theme pair interaction beyond count buckets.
- Rival Genre/Theme selection, detailed legal hand policy, Store acquisition and entrant behavior; a market formula alone does not make two weak trial rivals challenging.
- Board size/placement/tie rule and sales unit versus revenue detail; threshold/placement of separate following-month high-Review release news.
- CPU/save/history bounds and representative player/AI challenge target. Compare matched legal states and action tapes with market effects on/off, including crowded pair, Genre-only, Theme-only, unoccupied route, self-competition and no-competitor controls. Measure frozen Review standards, units, cents, Fans, AI solvency, chronology and visible explanation. Final tuning requires player-facing playtests.
