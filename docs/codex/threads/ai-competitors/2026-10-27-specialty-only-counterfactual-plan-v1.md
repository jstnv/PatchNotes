# Specialty-only ceiling counterfactual — pre-run plan, 2026-10-27

The [eight-Genre ceiling finding](2026-10-27-eight-genre-capability-ceiling-finding-v1.md) shows 28 high-Review games from eight equal-pool studios, but 17 high games use a non-specialty Genre. Action, Puzzle, Racing and Strategy have zero 8.0+ games in their own Genre. The diagnostic Genre chooser could be avoiding an attainable specialty success or correctly detecting a weak Core fit.

**Hypothesis:** forcing later projects to the studio's own specialty with the same broad owned pool will reveal which Genres can actually earn 8.0+ by the 72-cycle career boundary. This is a deliberately rejected-policy counterfactual: the LOCKED rule says rivals must choose a Genre suited to their funded, jointly attainable Core plan and may not be forced into a poor specialty project. It supplies evidence about capability, not a proposed AI behavior.

Create private G as a child of receipt variant F. Change only `_funded_core_studio_choice`'s project `genre` assignment to the permanent specialty. Keep the equal synthetic 27-Primitive ownership snapshot, initial cash/traits/RNG, Store/Bank/Feature/QA choices, Review targets and 72-cycle/max300 decision horizon unchanged. Compare F and G on seeds `4417`,`4423`,`4431`, eight independent solo studios each. Verify equal initial state/RNG and first release per studio. Measure all later Review/Core/Scope/Bugs/dates, 8.0+ in own Genre, release count, Store payments, loans, final cash, blocked/failed cases and actual chosen Genre receipts. Same seeds do not guarantee the same later draws or finances after a Genre choice changes, so compare complete legal trajectories, not a formula substitution. One 50-command smoke, 240-second process bound, one fresh replay and one cold repeat. Stop on invalid source capture/illegal action/timeout; retain failures.
